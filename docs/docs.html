How it works

WinMore is two programs. On Windows, a small companion owns everything: it plays your music, holds your logins, talks to Discord and Windows audio, and runs the apps. On your iPhone or Android phone, a native app turns sideways into a control surface for it.

The phone never runs an app's logic. Each app sends the phone a screen described in JSON, the phone draws it with native components, and taps come back to the PC as commands: {module, action, value}. That is why installing or updating an app never needs a new phone build, and why every app works the same on iPhone and Android.

The PC does the work and the phone shows it. Close the phone app and the music keeps playing on the PC.

Install on Windows

  1. Download WinMore-Setup-<version>.exe from the latest release. The installer is not signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may ask you to confirm.
  2. Run it. WinMore installs for your user (no admin needed), adds desktop and Start menu shortcuts, and starts.
  3. The island appears as a black pill at the top right of your main screen. Allow WinMore through the firewall on private networks when Windows asks: the phone reaches it over your network.

Uninstall it from Windows Settings → Apps, like anything else.

Pair your phone

  1. Install the phone app: on Android, download WinMore-Android-<version>.apk from the latest release on the phone and open it (Android asks once to allow installing from your browser); on iPhone, build it from source with Xcode for now.
  2. Put the phone and the PC on the same trusted Wi-Fi or LAN, and open WinMore on the phone. Allow local network access.
  3. In the phone's Settings, pick your PC under Nearby PCs and request pairing.
  4. The island opens with a six-digit code. Type it on the phone within two minutes (five tries). Decline on the island if you didn't ask for it.
  5. That's it: the phone remembers the PC and reconnects by itself, even when its address changes. Rotate to landscape.

To unpair everything, use Forget phones in the island's settings: the PC gets a new key and every phone has to pair again.

The island

The island is WinMore's face on the PC: always on top, OLED black, and out of the way until you need it. The menu at the top of this site is a copy of it.

  • Move it: drag the pill (or the expanded header). It snaps to one of eight docks, the corners and edge midpoints, on any monitor, and remembers where you left it.
  • Hide it: the × button dismisses it while music and the phone keep working. Bring it back with Ctrl+Alt+W or the tray icon.
  • Type from the PC: every text field on the phone has a keyboard icon. Tap it and the island opens Typing to phone: what you type on the PC appears in the phone field live. Enter submits, Esc stops.
  • Notifications from apps: apps can briefly take over the island, like Clipboard showing something sent from the phone with Copy and Dismiss. It goes back to your music after 30 seconds.

The phone app

A native app with a rail of your apps on the side, built for landscape (portrait works too). It keeps the screen awake while open.

  • iPhone: SwiftUI. On iOS 26 and later its floating controls use Liquid Glass; content sits on flat system surfaces.
  • Android: Jetpack Compose with Material 3 Expressive: a floating toolbar, tonal buttons and Material's own sliders, switches and sheets, colored from each app's accent. Android 10 or later.

Both draw the same screens: an app is written once, and each phone gives it its own native look.

Pick which apps sit in the rail in the phone's Settings → Navigation. Every screen in the app, the built-in ones included, is drawn from the JSON its app sends, so new apps show up on the phone as soon as they're installed on the PC.

Updates

  • WinMore for Windows checks a few seconds after launch and every six hours. When there's a new release, the island shows Update to <version>: one click downloads it, installs it silently and restarts.
  • Apps update themselves from the catalog (Settings → Apps → Update apps automatically), or one by one with Update and Check now.
  • The Android app shows Update to <version> in Settings when a new build exists; it downloads the APK and Android installs it over the current one.
  • The iPhone app shows a blue dot on Settings when a new build exists. Ad Hoc builds install in place; builds from Xcode are reinstalled from Xcode.

Network and privacy

  • Everything stays on your network. The PC listens on port 4317 and announces itself over Bonjour as _winmore._tcp.
  • The pairing key is random and stored encrypted with your Windows account. Logins (YouTube Music, Reddit) live in WinMore's own browser profile on the PC; secrets like Discord's never reach the phone.
  • Apps run inside WinMore with its access to your PC. The catalog is published only by its owner; for anything from elsewhere, WinMore asks before installing.

The catalog

Every feature in WinMore is an app, installed from Settings → Apps on the island. Here's what each one does and what it needs.

Build an app

An app is a folder. It runs on the PC; its phone screen is JSON each phone draws natively. Copy this, change the names, and you have an app.

hello/                     the folder name must equal the app id
├── manifest.json          id, name, version, entry, "ui": "kit"
├── index.cjs              activate(): data() and command(), runs on the PC
├── lib/…                  any other backend code (only the PC sees it)
├── ui/
│   ├── screen.json        the phone screen, built from Kit components
│   └── chart.html         optional web blocks
├── assets/…               images, fonts, video the screen uses
└── icon.svg · icon.png    the app's glyph

Only ui/, assets/ and the icons reach the phone. Add "public": ["ui", "assets", "media"] to the manifest to share other folders.

manifest.json

{
  "id": "hello",
  "name": "Hello",
  "shortName": "Hello",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "description": "What it does, in one sentence.",
  "entry": "index.cjs",
  "ui": "kit",
  "screen": "ui/screen.json",
  "symbol": "hand.wave",
  "icon": "icon.svg",
  "iconPNG": "icon.png"
}

symbol is the SF Symbol the phone shows until iconPNG arrives. platforms: ["win32"] limits an app to Windows. icon is a black SVG glyph (WinMore shows it grey); npm run render:icons makes the white 120 px PNG from it.

The backend: index.cjs

function activate(ctx){
  // ctx.dataDir: a folder for this app's files. ctx.electron, ctx.log, ctx.app.version too.
  let count = 0;
  return {
    data(context){ return { count, items: [] }; },   // what the screen's bindings read
    command(action, value){                          // what the screen's actions send
      if (action === 'add') count += value;
      return count;
    },
    close(){}                                        // stop timers, close windows
  };
}
module.exports = { activate };
  • data() is called while your screen is open, every 1.5 s by default. The PC only sends it when it changed; for big data, include a revision that changes when the data does.
  • command() may be async. What it returns reaches the phone. Throw an Error with a readable message and the phone shows it.
  • Keep slow work in the background and report progress through data() (a loading flag), so commands answer quickly.
  • Optional: state() adds keys for the PC island, settings.describe() gives the app its own category in the island's settings, and ctx.island.present() briefly takes over the island.

The screen

ui/screen.json describes the phone screen from Kit components. Here's a complete one:

{
  "type": "screen",
  "title": "Hello",
  "chrome": "fullscreen",
  "accent": "teal",
  "state": { "query": "" },
  "bar": {
    "leading": [{ "type": "label", "text": "$pc", "symbol": "desktopcomputer", "glass": true, "shape": "capsule", "padding": [0, 14], "height": 42 }],
    "trailing": [{ "type": "button", "variant": "glass", "symbol": "plus", "action": { "command": "add", "value": 1 } }]
  },
  "body": {
    "type": "scroll",
    "children": [
      { "type": "each", "items": "$items", "as": "item",
        "template": { "type": "row", "title": "$item.title", "subtitle": "{{item.created|age}}", "accessory": "chevron",
                      "action": { "cover": "detail", "params": "$item" } } }
    ]
  },
  "covers": { "detail": { "body": { "type": "text", "text": "$params.title", "style": "title2" } } }
}
  • Design rules: Liquid Glass ("glass": true, "variant": "glass") only for floating controls in the bar and over media. Content sits on flat card and section surfaces.
  • "chrome": "fullscreen" gives your app the whole content area. height: "screen" with a snapping scroll makes paging cards.
  • sheets and covers are named extra pages, opened by actions.

Bindings and actions

Bindings read data

Any value can come from your backend's data(): "$items", "$item.title", or a template like "{{count}} hellos" with filters such as {{size|bytes}}. Logic uses operator objects: {"if": [{"gt": ["$count", 0]}, "Some", "None"]}.

Screen variables

What only the phone cares about (is search open, the text in a field) lives in state, read as $ui.name and changed with {"set": …} or {"toggle": …}.

Actions run on taps

command (to your backend), sheet, cover, dismiss, open, set, haptic… A list runs in order; a failing command stops it and runs its onError. Controls put their new value in $value.

Optimistic updates

[
  { "set": "pending", "value": true },
  { "command": "save", "onError": { "set": "pending", "value": null } },
  { "set": "pending", "value": null }
]

…and show {"coalesce": ["$ui.pending", "$saved"]}. Every action, operator and filter is in the reference.

Web blocks

When the components aren't enough, drop in your own HTML: {"type": "web", "src": "ui/chart.html", "props": {"title": "Last hellos"}, "fill": "both"}. The page loads from your package and talks to the app:

winmore.onData(({ data, ui, props, params, env }) => render(data));  // now and on every change
const result = await winmore.command('add', 5);                       // like a command action
await winmore.set('query', 'cats');                                   // a screen variable
await winmore.run({ sheet: 'settings' });                             // any Kit action
winmore.open('https://example.com');                                  // the phone's browser

Pages are transparent, so glass controls float over them. If the same web block shows up in several apps, it's a good candidate for a new native component.

Develop and publish

  1. Open your apps folder from the island: Settings → Apps → Open folder (%APPDATA%\WinMore\apps) and drop your folder in. WinMore loads it right away and the phone adds it to its rail.
  2. Edit any file. WinMore reloads the app within a second and the phone reloads its screen. No restart.
  3. If the screen has a mistake, the phone says exactly what to fix. Add "$schema" pointing to screen.schema.json for autocomplete in your editor.
  4. Share it as a folder (Settings → Apps → Install folder…) or a .wmapp file. Bump version on every release so installed copies update.
